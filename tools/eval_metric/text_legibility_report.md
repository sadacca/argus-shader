# T-022 follow-up — text legibility vs. nearest-neighbour, explained

Generated by `text_legibility_diff.py`. See its module docstring for method. Answers the question `report.md`'s headline table raises but doesn't explain: on `letter_A`, argus-mobile-lite scores *below* nearest-neighbour on IoU (94.0% vs 94.2%) — is that a real text-legibility regression, or boundary noise between two equally-defensible reconstructions?

## letter_A

- 280 of 147456 pixels (0.19%) where argus-mobile-lite and nearest-neighbour disagree
- **106** of those are at pixels where nearest-neighbour's own blocky upscale already disagrees with ground truth (an inherently ambiguous anti-aliased boundary zone, not a regression)
- **174** are pixels where nearest-neighbour matches ground truth and argus-mobile-lite differs — the bucket that would actually indicate a legibility regression against the naive baseline. Of those, argus is still independently *correct* against ground truth for 0 (a case where two different upscalers both happen to land on the right side of a threshold differently) and genuinely wrong for **174**.

- **215** are pixels where nearest-neighbour matches ground truth and argus-mobile-lite differs — the bucket that would actually indicate a legibility regression against the naive baseline. Of those, argus is still independently *correct* against ground truth for 0 (a case where two different upscalers both happen to land on the right side of a threshold differently) and genuinely wrong for **215**.

## Reading this honestly

Visually auditing where the "interior" (genuinely-wrong-vs-ground-truth) pixels actually fall (`audit/letter_A_legibility_regression.png`, `audit/letter_g_legibility_regression.png` — wrong pixels painted red on the ground truth) shows they sit exactly on the glyphs' curved strokes and sharp corners (the A's apex and inner-triangle corners; the g's bowl curve and terminal), never in flat interior regions away from any stroke. This is not a classification failure scattering wrong pixels at random — it's T-018's edge-reconstruction sub-pixel blend making a different rounding choice than nearest-neighbour's blocky snap at these specific corner/curve locations, plausibly the same **compass-snapping limitation T-018's own report.md already documents** (the LUT's 8 discrete directions approximate a continuous curve or an off-compass corner angle imperfectly) rather than a new bug.

**Still, read plainly against T-022's actual wording**: "text legibility no worse than nearest-neighbour" is not literally true at the pixel level on this corpus — a small, consistently-located set of corner/curve pixels (174 on `letter_A`, 215 on `letter_g`, both ~0.1-0.6% of the image) are pixel-wrong where the naive nearest-neighbour baseline is pixel-right. Whether that constitutes a real legibility problem is a readability judgment call this metric can't make on its own (sub-pixel corner rounding on a large supersampled test glyph is a different question from perceived legibility of small pixel-art text in practice, which is what FR2/T-017 actually targets) — flagged honestly as new scope rather than either asserted as passing or overclaimed as a serious defect.
